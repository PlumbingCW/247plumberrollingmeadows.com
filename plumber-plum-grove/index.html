What We Mean by the Plum Grove Area

Plum Grove is an old name in this part of Cook County. Wikipedia lists Plum Grove as an early land-claim area, Plum Grove Road is a main route, and Plum Grove Junior High is a Rolling Meadows school. Several communities carry the name today, including Plum Grove Village, Plum Grove Creek, Plum Grove Hills and Plum Grove Countryside.

A 2012 Daily Herald neighborhood profile places Plum Grove Village north of Kirchoff Road, south of Euclid Avenue and east of Plum Grove Road, entered from Duxbury. A real estate listing site puts Plum Grove Creek south of Euclid Avenue and east of Quentin Road. The sources do not agree on every detail, so we describe the area by those anchor roads only.

A Mix of Townhomes and Single-Family Streets

The Daily Herald profile describes Plum Grove Village as townhouses built in the 1960s, mostly ranches with basements and garages, with the community handling snow and lawn care. Listing data for Plum Grove Creek gives build years of 1976 to 1984 for single-family homes. That data comes from a real estate site and is supporting evidence, not an official record.

The two housing types raise different plumbing questions. A single-family owner decides everything from the meter to the faucets. A townhome owner often shares walls, drain stacks or supply lines with neighbors, and the community rules decide who handles what.

Water Heaters in Townhomes and Small Closets

Tank heaters in closets and utility rooms are common in townhomes. Many tank heaters last roughly eight to twelve years, so one that has been in place for a decade is worth watching for rust at the base, damp floors or rumbling noises.

The City of Rolling Meadows says a new or replacement water heater needs a permit, so ask whoever replaces yours how that will be handled. The water heater repair page explains what a plumber checks before recommending a swap, and water heater installation covers new units.

Creek, Reservoir and Drainage Context

The comprehensive plan says the Plum Grove Reservoir, west of Quentin Road near Harper College, and the Twin Lakes Reservoir provide flood control benefits. Those are regional facilities, and the plan does not say they keep any particular street dry.

It also describes Salt Creek as part of the city's stormwater system. We make no claim that homes in the Plum Grove area flood more or less than elsewhere. If your basement is wet, the cause is worth testing, and the city's Residential Drainage Assistance Program may apply to owner-occupied single-family homes.
