What Hydro Jetting Does That a Cable Cannot

A drain cable is a spinning blade on a steel line. It cuts through the middle of a blockage and pulls some of it back, which restores flow but leaves a coating of grease and scale on the pipe wall. The coating narrows the pipe, and the clog returns.

A hydro jetter uses a hose and a nozzle that fires water backward to scrub the whole pipe wall and drive debris toward the exit. The result is a pipe that is close to its original diameter instead of a drain with a pinhole in the middle.

When Hydro Jetting Is an Emergency

If the main line is full and water is coming up through a floor drain, tub or toilet, the line needs to be opened today. A jetter can clear a packed line faster than repeated cable runs, which cuts the time that sewage sits in the house.

Restaurants and multi-unit buildings feel this more sharply, because grease and a heavy water load fill a line quickly. Our commercial plumbing page covers after-hours calls for those properties.

Signs a Line Needs Jetting

Slow drains after a cable visit

The clog is gone but the pipe wall is still coated, so the line drains slowly and backs up again within weeks.

Gurgling toilets and tubs

Air is being pushed back through traps because the main line is partially blocked.

Sewer smell near drains

Grease and organic buildup in the line decays and produces odor.

Repeat clogs in one line

The same line has needed clearing more than once in a year, which points to scale, grease or roots.

Cable or Jetter: Choosing the Right Tool

FactorCable machineHydro jetter
Best forSoft clogs, hair, paperGrease, scale, sand, light roots
ResultOpens a channel through the clogCleans the full pipe wall
Pipe conditionFine for most pipeBest checked by camera first on fragile or collapsed pipe
How long it lastsWeeks to months if the wall stays coatedOften longer, because the buildup is removed

Jetting is not right for every line. A pipe with a collapse, a deep offset or a broken joint needs a camera look first, and sometimes a sewer line repair instead of more water pressure.

Roots, Grease and Older Lines in Rolling Meadows

A lot of the housing in Rolling Meadows was built between 1950 and 1980, according to the city's 2018 comprehensive plan, and mature trees line many streets. Roots search for water and enter lateral pipes at joints, which is why the city's own sewer guidance mentions keeping trees away from the lateral line and recommends periodic rodding from a six-inch cleanout.

Jetting can cut light root growth, but heavy roots may return unless the joint is repaired. A camera pass after jetting shows whether the line is clean and whether a repair is worth considering. That visual proof is also useful if you need to show a landlord or insurer what was done.
