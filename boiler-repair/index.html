Boiler Emergency: Safety First

If you smell gas, do not touch switches, leave the building and call 911 or the gas utility. If you suspect carbon monoxide, get everyone outside and call 911, since CO is odorless and dangerous. A working CO alarm on each floor is a basic safeguard.

If the boiler is leaking, turn off its power and close the cold water feed. If the pipes are at risk of freezing, tell dispatch so the visit is prioritized. Take a photo of the nameplate with the model, which helps the plumber bring parts.

Boilers in Homes and Multi-Unit Buildings

Many multifamily buildings in Rolling Meadows are three or four stories, per the city's comprehensive plan, and about a third of the city's housing is rented. Central boilers serve several units, so an outage affects many people, and property managers often call after hours.

If you manage a building, see the commercial plumbing page for how after-hours service and shared systems are handled. Tenants should call their manager first, and the manager can authorize the repair.

Venting, Clearances and Permits

The City of Rolling Meadows plumbing FAQ states that single-wall vent pipe needs 6 inches of clearance to combustibles and B-vent needs 1 inch. Gas appliances in tight spaces also need proper combustion air. These rules matter after a replacement and not just a repair.

A permit is required for a new or replacement water heater, and the city may require one for other mechanical work. The plumber tells you what applies before you approve the price. If the repair is beyond a plumber's scope, such as a gas line issue, we tell you who to call.

Keeping a Boiler Running

  • Schedule an annual service before the heating season
  • Check the pressure gauge monthly and note changes
  • Keep the area around the boiler clear
  • Test CO alarms and replace batteries
  • Bleed radiators if they are cold at the top
