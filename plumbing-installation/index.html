What Installation Covers

Typical installation jobs include toilets, sinks, faucets, tubs and showers, laundry hookups, dishwashers, garbage disposals, water heaters and new shutoff valves. For larger projects the plumber may also run new supply and drain lines, relocate fixtures or add a bathroom.

The best time to bring in a plumber on a remodel is before the walls are closed up. Rough-in plumbing is cheaper to change while the framing is open, and a plan that includes shutoffs at each fixture saves emergency calls later.

Code, Permits and Inspection in Rolling Meadows

The City of Rolling Meadows requires contractor licenses and bonds to be on file with Community Development before a permit is issued. Its plumbing FAQ includes a few rules that affect installations: dishwashers may not discharge into a disposal, concealed plumbing needs an access door or panel, and single-wall vent pipe needs 6 inches of clearance to combustibles while B-vent needs 1 inch.

Illinois plumbing code is set in 77 Ill. Adm. Code 890, and municipal inspectors enforce it. Before you buy fixtures or appliances, ask the plumber whether a permit applies. Doing it before work starts avoids having to open a finished wall for an inspection.

Choosing Fixtures That Fit the House

Older Rolling Meadows homes often have smaller bathrooms and compact kitchens, since much of the housing dates from 1950 to 1980. Measure the toilet rough-in, the vanity depth and the sink cabinet before you buy. A fixture that does not fit costs time and a return trip.

Ask for a shutoff valve at every new fixture. A small valve under a sink or behind a toilet lets you isolate a problem without shutting off the whole house, and it is one of the cheapest upgrades a plumber can add during an install.

Questions to Ask Before an Install

  • Is a permit needed for this job, and who pulls it?
  • Are the existing shutoffs and supply lines in good shape or should they be replaced?
  • What is included in the price, and what would change it?
  • Will walls or floors need to be opened, and who repairs them?
  • What happens if a leak shows up after the job?
