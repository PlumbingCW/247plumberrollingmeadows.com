Sewer Camera Inspection in Rolling Meadows, IL

A sewer camera inspection sends a small waterproof camera through your sewer lateral so you can see cracks, roots, bellies and breaks before you spend money on a repair. It is a planned service, usually booked for a same-day or next-day slot when available, and it is also worth having after a backup.

What a Sewer Camera Inspection Shows

The plumber feeds a flexible cable with a camera head into the line from the cleanout. As it travels toward the street, the camera streams video to a monitor, and a locator can mark where the camera sits underground. You see the pipe material, the joints and anything that does not belong in it.

Typical findings include roots entering at joints, cracks, an offset joint where two sections no longer line up, a belly where the pipe sags and holds water, and scale or grease coating the wall. Each one points to a different next step.

Reasons to Schedule an Inspection

Buying or selling a home

The lateral is the homeowner's responsibility from the building to the city main, so you want to know its condition before closing.

After a backup

A camera run tells you whether the backup was a one-time clog or a sign of damage.

Before a repair

Seeing the line lets the plumber choose between spot repair, lining and replacement with facts.

Repeat clogs

If the same drain clogs every few months, the camera finds the cause that cabling cannot.

How the Visit Works

  1. 1

    Find the access

    The plumber locates the cleanout or removes a toilet or trap to reach the line.

  2. 2

    You get an explanation of the findings, plus a recording or notes you can keep for a repair decision or a landlord.

Inspections for Rolling Meadows Homes

Most Rolling Meadows housing was built between 1950 and 1980, according to the city's 2018 comprehensive plan, so the sewer laterals under those lawns have been in the ground a long time. A camera inspection does not tell you the pipe's age, but it shows its condition, which matters more.

The city's sewer guide makes the homeowner responsible for the lateral up to the city main, and it requires a permit before most work and a six-inch cleanout before the city will start right-of-way work. An inspection before a repair gives you what you need to talk through those steps.

A Note on Home Purchases

A standard home inspection does not usually include a sewer camera run. If you are buying an older home in Rolling Meadows, ask whether the seller has a recent recording, or schedule your own before the inspection period ends. The recording gives you leverage and a clearer budget.

We do not make promises about what the camera will find. We tell you what we see and what it could mean, and you decide how to proceed.
