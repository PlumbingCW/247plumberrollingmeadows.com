Items a Proper Install Includes

A code-compliant install is more than the heater. It includes a temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge pipe, shutoff valves, a way to drain the unit and correct venting for gas units. Where the plumbing is a closed system, an expansion tank may be needed to absorb pressure from heated water.

The Illinois Plumbing Code, published as 77 Illinois Administrative Code 890, sets the minimum standard, and municipal plumbing inspectors check work against it. We install to that code and tell you what is required on your job.

Rolling Meadows Permit and Inspection Notes

The City of Rolling Meadows requires a permit for a new or replacement water heater, according to its FAQ, and the permit is issued by Community Development, which wants the contractor's license and bond on file. Plan the permit into your schedule, since an inspection may follow.

The city's plumbing guidance also states that single-wall vent pipe needs six inches of clearance, B-vent needs one inch and concealed plumbing must be reachable through an access panel. We build those into the plan so the installation passes the first time.

Where to Put the New Heater

Many Rolling Meadows homes from the 1950s through the 1970s have the heater in a utility closet or basement corner with limited clearance. A larger or tankless unit may not fit the old footprint, so measurements matter before you buy.

If you are remodeling, tell us before the walls close. Moving a heater later costs far more than planning it now, and the same visit can handle related work. See plumbing installation for fixtures and lines that often go with it.
