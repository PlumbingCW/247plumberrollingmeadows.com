Where Kimball Hill Is and Where the Name Comes From

Kimball Hill was a person before it was a place name. Developer Kimball Hill bought land in the early 1950s, advertised a model home in the Chicago Tribune and named the new community Rolling Meadows. The first residents moved in during 1953, and the city incorporated in 1955.

The city's 2018 comprehensive plan describes the original Kimball Hill neighborhoods as well planned and well maintained, with mature trees and nearby parks and schools. A 2015 Daily Herald feature says the first houses went up along Campbell Street and Wilke Road.

No official map draws a line around Kimball Hill, and we will not pretend to know one. We use the name for the early residential streets, and Kimball Hill Park at 3266 Kirchoff Road is a handy landmark. If you are unsure whether your street fits, call and give us the cross streets.

What the Age of These Homes Means for Plumbing

The plan says most of the city's housing was built between 1950 and 1980, and that the majority of homes are single-family. That tells you when the houses went up. It does not tell you what is inside a particular wall, since many owners have remodeled kitchens and baths over seventy years.

A plumber has to look to know. A slow main drain, a tank heater that has been running for many years or a fixture that no longer shuts off all point to a visit, not to a guess. We do not assume your home has any specific pipe material, and we will tell you what we actually find.

Who Maintains the Sewer Line From Your House

The City of Rolling Meadows owns the sewer main. According to its City of Rolling Meadows Sanitary Sewer Problem Guide, the homeowner is responsible for the private lateral that runs from the building to the main, including the part under the easement or right-of-way.

The city suggests rodding from an outside 6 inch cleanout with a root cutter every two to three years, or more often if you have had problems. Most sewer work needs a city permit, and the city will not start a right-of-way repair until a 6 inch cleanout is installed or rodding has been tried.

The guide also says to call Public Works before you call a plumber when a sewer backs up, because a city representative will check the main line at no cost. Public Works is at 847-963-0500.

Before You Call About a Backup

A few quick steps help whoever comes to your door.

Stop the water

Hold off on sinks, showers, laundry and flushing while a drain is backing up.

Find the cleanout

The city guide says to locate your 6 inch cleanout and keep the cap on and in good condition.

Note the pattern

Does it back up after rain, after laundry or all the time? Say so on the call.

Call Public Works first

The city checks the main line before a contractor works on yours.

Then call (847) 281-8626 if the city finds the main clear and the problem is on your side.

Wet Basements and Standing Water

The comprehensive plan lists stormwater infrastructure among local needs and describes Salt Creek as part of the city's stormwater system. It does not name Kimball Hill as a flood area, and neither will we. Wet basements usually have a cause you can test, such as a failing pump, a clogged drain or water pooling against the foundation.

The city runs a Residential Drainage Assistance Program for owner-occupied single-family homes where standing water stays 48 hours or more after rain. It can cover up to 40 percent of project costs, capped at $2,000, and applications must come from at least three adjoining properties. It is citywide and not limited to Kimball Hill, so ask Public Works about eligibility.
