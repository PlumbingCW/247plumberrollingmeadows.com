Water Softener and Filtration in Rolling Meadows, IL

Water softeners and filters are planned installs, but they are often prompted by scale on fixtures, spotted glasses or a taste concern. Before buying anything, test your tap and ask the city for its annual water quality report. Call us to talk through what you see and what a unit would involve.

Start With the Water You Actually Have

The City of Rolling Meadows buys Lake Michigan water that is treated in Chicago and delivered through the Northwest Suburban Municipal Joint Action Water Agency, according to its 2025 water quality report. The city does not list a hardness value in that report, so we do not quote one.

A hardness test kit from a hardware store or a lab test will tell you the number at your tap. The city's Public Works department can send the annual report, and it is the right place to ask questions about the supply. Spend nothing on equipment until you have a real reading.

Lead and Household Plumbing

The City of Rolling Meadows states it has no known lead service lines, and its 2023 lead monitoring showed a 90th percentile of 0.9 parts per billion. The city also says lead can enter water from household plumbing and fixtures. If your home has older fixtures or solder, a test is the way to know.

If you want a filter for lead, look for one certified for lead reduction, and ask the plumber about proper installation and replacement of cartridges. The water service line repair page explains who owns which part of the line.

Things to Know About Softeners

  • Softeners use salt or potassium and need periodic refills
  • They require a drain connection and an electrical outlet
  • Bypass valves let you use untreated water for outdoor taps
  • Softened water can affect people on low-sodium diets, so ask a doctor
  • Regular service keeps resin and valves working

Service and Repair of Existing Units

If you already have a softener or filter, common problems are salt bridges, clogged injectors, a failed control valve and old cartridges. A plumber can inspect the unit, flush the system and tell you whether repair or replacement makes sense.

If you notice a change in pressure after installing a unit, tell the plumber. A clogged filter or a partly closed bypass can reduce flow. The plumbing installation page covers related work.
