A Water Line Break: First Steps

Signs of a broken service line include water bubbling up in the yard, a sudden drop in pressure, muddy water and a meter that spins. Shut off the main inside the house to stop flow into the house, but keep in mind that water may still leak outdoors from the line up to that valve.

Then work out which side of the property the break is on. If water is surfacing near the street, near the meter pit or at the curb, call Rolling Meadows Public Works at 847-963-0500 on weekdays. If the break is on the home side, call our 24/7 line.

That split comes from the city's lead service line page. Knowing it can keep you from paying to repair something that is the city's responsibility.

What the City Says About Lead

The City of Rolling Meadows states that it has no known lead service lines. Its most recent lead monitoring results, from 2023, showed a 90th percentile of 0.9 parts per billion against an action level of 15, with no sites over the limit, and the city tests 30 sites every three years. It also says lead can enter water from household plumbing and fixtures, not from the source water.

The city is building an inventory map of service line materials. If your line shows as unknown, the city asks you to report it through its service line survey, and you can ask Public Works about it at 847-963-0500. A plumber who replaces your side of the line will note the pipe material, which can help close the gap.

Signs Your Service Line Needs Work

Low pressure at every fixture

A partially failed or corroded line limits flow into the house.

Wet or soft ground between meter and house

Water is escaping from the line underground.

Dirty or discolored water

Corroded pipe sheds rust into the supply.

Meter moves with everything off

A leak somewhere past the meter, which may be the service line.

Low Pressure Is Not Always the Service Line

Low pressure can come from the service line, but also from a failing pressure regulator, a partly closed valve, corroded galvanized pipe inside the house or a leak. The plumber checks pressure at an outdoor hose bib and compares it with the pressure inside, then works from there.

If the inside pipe is the problem, the repair belongs on the repiping page. If the line is fine and the pressure is simply high, a regulator may be the fix. We do not recommend a line replacement until we have measured.

Before Any Digging

Any excavation needs utility locates, so call 811 before digging near a buried line. A city permit may also apply for work in the right of way, and the city's Public Works department can tell you the rules for work near the street.

We lay out those steps before you approve the price, and we tell you if work on the city side is outside what we can do. You then know what is ours, what is yours and what belongs to Public Works.
