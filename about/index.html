Why We Built This Site Around Rolling Meadows

Rolling Meadows was incorporated in 1955, and the first residents moved into Kimball Hill homes in 1953. The city's 2018 comprehensive plan says most of its housing was built between 1950 and 1980. That puts a lot of plumbing at an age where supply lines, drain stacks and water heaters start to fail.

Local detail changes the advice. The city buys Lake Michigan water through the Northwest Suburban Municipal Joint Action Water Agency, owns the water service line up to the shutoff valve and asks residents to call Public Works before a plumber for sewer main blockages. We write our pages around those facts, so what you read matches the place you live.

What To Tell Dispatch When You Call

Start with the address and the thing that is wrong. Then say what you have shut off, whether water is still moving and whether anyone is in danger. If sewage is involved, say so, and say which fixtures are affected.

The more specific the description, the better the plumber can prepare for the visit and bring the right tools and parts. A photo of the water heater label or the pipe under the sink is useful if you can take one, and you can describe it over the phone while you wait. Tell dispatch if you rent, so the right person can approve the work.

How to Check Any Plumber in Illinois

Illinois licenses plumbers through the Illinois Department of Public Health. The state offers an online license verification tool that works for any plumber or plumbing contractor, ours included. The state also publishes the Illinois Plumbing Code as 77 Illinois Administrative Code 890, and licensed plumbing inspectors employed by municipalities check work against it.

It is a good habit to use that tool before you let anyone work on your plumbing. It takes a couple of minutes and it protects you whether you hire us or someone else.
