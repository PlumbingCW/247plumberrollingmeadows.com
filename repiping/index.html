Repiping in Rolling Meadows, IL

Repiping means replacing the supply pipes that carry water through a house. It is a planned project, but the signs that you need one often show up as an emergency, such as a burst pipe or brown water. Call the 24/7 line, describe what you see, and a plumber will check the pipe where it enters the house.

Signs Your Pipes Are Near the End

Low pressure at several fixtures

Galvanized steel narrows from the inside as mineral and rust build up.

Brown or orange water

Rust is flaking off the inside of old iron or steel pipe.

Repeat leaks in different places

One failure after another points to a pipe-wide problem and not a single defect.

Pinhole leaks or green stains on copper

Corrosion is thinning the wall.

Visible rust or flaking at joints

Threaded joints in old galvanized lines fail first.

Water that takes a long time to run hot

Restricted or leaking lines can slow the flow.

Why Repiping Comes Up in Rolling Meadows

Most of Rolling Meadows housing was built between 1950 and 1980, according to the city's comprehensive plan. Homes from that era used galvanized steel or copper for the supply side, and both have a working life. Galvanized steel corrodes from the inside, so a pipe that looks fine outside can be badly narrowed within.

We do not assume what is in any particular house. The plumber checks the pipe where it enters, looks at a few visible runs and tests pressure, then tells you whether you have a repair, a partial repipe or a whole-house project. That check is the starting point for any price.

How a Repipe Works

  1. 1

    Inspect and plan

    The plumber maps the existing lines and chooses a route that limits wall openings.

  2. 2

    Price and approve

    You get a clear price before work starts, including any permit and drywall repair.

  3. 3

    Replace the lines

    New pipe is run to fixtures and connected to the main and the water heater.

  4. 4

    Test and restore

    The system is pressure tested, then openings are patched or left ready for a finisher.

Partial or Whole-House

If one section is failing and the rest is sound, a partial repipe can solve the problem for less. If corrosion is widespread, fixing section by section means repeated wall openings and repeat water damage, and a whole-house repipe usually costs less over time.

The plumber can mark the sections that are in poor shape so you see why a partial repipe is or is not enough. If you are mid-emergency, the first step is a repair to stop the leak, and the repipe plan can follow later. The burst pipe repair page covers that first step.

Permits and What the City Says

The City of Rolling Meadows requires contractor licenses and bonds to be on file with Community Development before a permit is issued, and a permit is required for many plumbing projects. The city also says concealed plumbing needs an access door or panel, and that the supply pipe in copper or galvanized form must be type M or heavier.

Ask the plumber how permits and inspection fit into the schedule before you approve. Illinois sets the underlying plumbing code in 77 Ill. Adm. Code 890, enforced by municipal inspectors, so the finished work is inspected against it.

Before the Repipe Starts

  • Clear access to the basement, water heater area and under-sink cabinets
  • Plan for water to be off for part of the day and arrange for a place to wash
  • Move furniture and photographs away from walls that will be opened
  • Ask how drywall and paint will be handled and by whom
  • Check with your insurer, since some policies cover repiping after a sudden leak
