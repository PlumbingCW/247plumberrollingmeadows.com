Burst Pipe Repair: What To Do First in Rolling Meadows, IL

A burst pipe can release a lot of water in a short time, and the first ten minutes decide how bad the damage gets. This guide gives the order of steps, explains how pros repair the pipe and covers what to document for insurance.

By the Rolling Meadows 24/7 Plumbing team · Updated November 2026

    The First Ten Minutes

    1. 1

      Shut off the main

      Turn the main valve clockwise to stop the flow. If it is stuck, call for help and keep clear of the water.

    2. 2

      Kill the power if needed

      If water is near outlets or the panel, do not step into it. Call the electric utility or 911.

    3. 3

      Drain the lines

      Open the lowest faucet and flush an upstairs toilet to relieve pressure.

    4. 4

      Move and photograph

      Lift belongings off the floor and photograph damage before cleanup.

    5. 5

      Call a plumber

      Tell dispatch where the water is coming from and what is already off.

    Why Pipes Burst

    Freezing is the most famous cause, because water expands as it turns to ice. The pipe often splits at the point where pressure peaks, which may not be where the ice sits. Pipes in exterior walls, garages, crawlspaces and unheated basements are most exposed.

    Corrosion, high pressure and failed fittings also cause bursts. A burst in old galvanized pipe can mean the rest of the line is weak too, which is worth discussing with the plumber before deciding between repair and replacement.

    Drying and Documenting

    Water damage continues after the pipe is fixed. Remove standing water, open windows and run fans. Mold can start in damp materials within days, so dry quickly and watch for musty smells.

    Keep receipts, take photos of the damage and note the date and time. Many homeowners policies cover sudden bursts and their damage, but not slow leaks. Ask your insurer what it needs. A plumber can note the cause on the invoice.

    Prepare Before the Next Cold Snap

    • Find and test your main shutoff now
    • Insulate exposed pipes in garages and crawlspaces
    • Keep heat on, even in empty rooms
    • Let a faucet drip during a hard freeze
    • Know who to call after hours

    Common Places Pipes Burst

    Pipes in unheated spaces fail first. These include crawlspaces, garages, exterior walls, attics and the runs that pass near a bad window or a vent. In houses with a basement, the pipe near the rim joist where the wall meets the foundation is a frequent spot.

    Outdoor hose bibs are another weak point. A hose left attached through winter traps water that freezes and splits the faucet or the pipe behind it. Removing hoses and shutting off the indoor valve to outdoor faucets prevents that.

    What a Good Repair Looks Like

    A proper repair uses the right fittings for the pipe type, supports the pipe so it does not sag and keeps joints out of tight spots where they will be hard to reach. The plumber should repressurize the system and watch for leaks before closing any opening.

    Ask about the pipe on either side of the failure. Pipe near a burst has often been stretched or weakened, and replacing a few extra feet is cheaper than a second repair next month.

    After The Repair: Preventing A Repeat

    Once the pipe is fixed, look at why it failed. Insulate exposed runs, seal air leaks near pipes, add heat to cold spaces and replace corroded sections. Small steps now can prevent a second burst.

    Consider a smart water shutoff or a leak sensor near the water heater, sinks and the basement. These devices alert you or close the supply when they detect water, and they can limit the damage if a pipe fails when you are away.

    Keeping Records For Your Insurer

    Take wide photos of each affected room and close photos of the damage. Keep a list of damaged items with approximate value, and save receipts for fans, drying equipment and temporary repairs.

    Ask your insurer how soon to report the loss and what repairs may start before an adjuster visits. Clear records speed up a claim and reduce disagreements about what the water damaged.

    Ask whether your insurer has a preferred contractor for drying and repairs, and whether the plumber must document the cause before work begins. A short call early can save a lot of time after the water is gone.
