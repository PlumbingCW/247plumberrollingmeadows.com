Bathroom Sink Not Draining but Not Clogged in Rolling Meadows, IL

A sink that drains slowly even though you see no clog is common. The blockage is often hiding in the stopper, the trap or the pipe behind the wall, and sometimes the vent is the problem. This guide walks through the checks in order, from the easiest to the most involved.

By the Rolling Meadows 24/7 Plumbing team · Updated November 2026

    Checks In Order

    1. 1

      Remove the stopper

      Pull or unscrew the pop-up stopper and clean off the hair and gunk that collects on it.

    2. 2

      Clean the drain opening

      Use a plastic drain tool to pull hair from the first few inches.

    3. 3

      Check the pivot rod

      Under the sink, a rod links the stopper to the lever. A loose nut can keep the stopper from opening.

    4. 4

      Clean the trap

      Place a bucket, loosen the slip nuts on the P-trap and clear the debris.

    5. 5

      Test the vent and branch

      If the trap is clean and the sink still drains slowly, the blockage is deeper or the vent is restricted.

    Why Chemical Cleaners Are a Poor Choice

    Chemical drain cleaners can damage older pipes and finishes, and they are hazardous if they splash. They also may not reach the real problem, which is often a mat of hair and soap scum on the trap or stopper. A mechanical clean is safer and more effective.

    If you used a chemical cleaner and the sink still does not drain, tell the plumber. The standing liquid is caustic, and a plumber should know before working on the drain.

    The Vent Question

    Every drain needs air behind the water, supplied by a vent pipe that goes up through the roof. A blocked vent makes drains slow and gurgle because the water cannot flow freely. Leaves, nests and ice can block a vent.

    A vent problem is usually not a homeowner job, since it involves the roof. If a clean trap and a cleared line do not fix the slow drain, the plumber can check the vent with a camera or by running a cable from the roof.

    Keep the Sink Draining

    • Clean the stopper every few weeks
    • Use a hair catcher in the basin
    • Flush with hot water after brushing and shaving
    • Avoid pouring oils or coffee grounds down the drain
    • Run water in unused sinks to keep traps full

    Overflow Holes And Hidden Buildup

    Most bathroom sinks have an overflow hole under the rim. Soap scum and hair build up in the channel behind it, and it can smell. A bottle brush and a mix of hot water and dish soap cleans it out.

    If gurgling comes from the overflow when the sink drains, it can also signal a partial blockage lower in the line. The overflow channel connects to the drain below the stopper, so it carries the same air and noise.

    Using A Drain Snake Safely

    A small hand snake can reach past the trap. Feed it in gently, turn the handle and pull the cable out slowly, because it may bring out a clump of hair. Wear gloves and expect it to be messy.

    Stop if the cable meets a hard stop or if it will not turn. Forcing it can scratch the fixture or push the clog deeper. At that point, a plumber has better tools and can look at the line with a camera.

    Hair, Soap And Mineral Buildup

    The inside of a drain pipe is rarely smooth. Soap forms a sticky film that catches hair, and mineral deposits narrow the opening over time. Even a pipe that is not fully blocked can drain slowly.

    Regular cleaning is the best prevention. A monthly pass with a plastic drain tool and a hot water flush keeps buildup from hardening, and you will rarely need anything stronger.

    Rentals And Shared Plumbing

    In an apartment, a slow sink can be a shared drain problem. Tell the manager promptly, mention if neighbors have the same issue and take a short video of the sink draining slowly. That evidence helps the manager decide who to send.

    Avoid pouring chemical cleaner into a shared drain. It can harm the pipes and puts maintenance workers at risk when they open the line.

    Take a photo of the trap and drain before you take them apart, so reassembly is easy. Replace worn washers while the trap is off, because a dry-fit joint that leaks later brings you back under the sink with a bucket.

    Older Homes in Rolling Meadows

    The City of Rolling Meadows 2018 comprehensive plan says most housing was built between 1950 and 1980, so many bathrooms have original drain stacks and older traps. Those pipes can be rough inside, which gives hair and soap more to catch on.

    If several fixtures are slow, the cause may be in the main line. The city's sewer guide asks residents to call Public Works at 847-963-0500 on weekdays before a plumber if the main may be blocked. If the main is clear, the problem is on the owner's side and we can help.

    When To Call a Plumber

    Call if the sink still drains slowly after the checks above, if other fixtures are affected or if you smell sewage. A plumber can cable the line, run a camera and fix the cause.
