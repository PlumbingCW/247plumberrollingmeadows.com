Toilet and Shower Clogged at the Same Time in Rolling Meadows, IL

When a toilet and a shower back up together, the clog is rarely in either fixture. They usually share a drain line, and the blockage sits downstream of both. This guide explains how to confirm that, what to do right away and who to call during the day and after hours.

By the Rolling Meadows 24/7 Plumbing team · Updated November 2026

    Why Two Fixtures Fail Together

    Every fixture in a house drains into a branch line that joins a larger drain. A clog in a branch affects only the fixtures on it. A clog in the main drain affects everything upstream of it, and the lowest fixtures, such as a basement shower or floor drain, show the problem first.

    A toilet and a shower in the same bathroom share a branch in many houses. If both are slow, the blockage could be in that bathroom's branch or in the main below it. Checking other fixtures in the house helps narrow it down.

    A Five-Minute Diagnosis

    1. 1

      Stop using water

      Pause showers, laundry and dishwashing so the line can settle.

    2. 2

      Check other fixtures

      Run a sink on another floor and flush a different toilet. If they drain normally, the clog is probably in the bathroom branch.

    3. 3

      Look at the lowest drain

      Check the basement floor drain and tub. Water or gurgling there points to the main.

    4. 4

      Listen for bubbling

      Gurgling in a tub when a toilet flushes means the vent or drain is restricted.

    5. 5

      Call with your findings

      Tell dispatch which fixtures are affected and what they do.

    Why Plunging May Not Help

    A plunger works on a clog near the fixture. If the blockage is in a branch or main line, pressure just moves water around. Plunging a toilet while sewage is coming up from a floor drain can push dirty water into the bathroom.

    Avoid running more water to see if it drains. Every gallon you add can end up on the floor. If water is already rising, stop using the plumbing and call.

    What Not To Do

    • Do not pour chemical drain cleaner into backed-up fixtures
    • Do not keep flushing to test the line
    • Do not touch standing sewage without gloves and boots
    • Do not use electrical devices near wet floors
    • Do not ignore a repeat problem, since the cause is likely still there

    How Roots and Pipe Damage Cause Repeat Backups

    Tree roots look for moisture and enter small gaps at pipe joints. Over the years they thicken and catch paper and grease, forming a net that slows and then stops flow. Cleaning removes the roots in the pipe, but the roots grow back until the joint is repaired.

    Clay pipe, and older cast iron, can also crack, offset or collapse. A camera inspection shows which problem you have, so you can decide between periodic cleaning and a repair that stops the backups for good.

    Protecting the House Until Help Arrives

    Keep people and pets out of any area with sewage, and wear boots and gloves if you must enter. Open windows for ventilation. Do not mix cleaning products, since some combinations make toxic gas.

    Photograph the water and any damaged items for your insurer. Some policies cover sewer backup only with an added endorsement, so it is worth reading your policy now and asking about the option before a backup happens.

    Questions Dispatch Will Ask

    Dispatch will ask which fixtures are affected, whether there is a basement or floor drain and whether sewage is on the floor. They will ask if anything unusual was flushed and how long the problem has lasted.

    Having those answers ready helps send the right equipment. A heavy main line blockage may need a jetter, while a branch clog needs a cable, and the plumber can arrive prepared for either.

    Preventing the Next Backup

    Keep grease, wipes, paper towels and hygiene products out of the drains. If you have trees near the sewer line, ask a plumber about periodic cleaning or a camera check, since roots return after cutting.

    If you rent, report slow drains early. A small clog is far easier to clear than a full backup, and early reports protect both you and the building.

    The City of Rolling Meadows asks residents to call Public Works before a plumber when a main line blockage is suspected.
