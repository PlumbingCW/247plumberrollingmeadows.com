Hot Water Heater Igniter Replacement in Rolling Meadows, IL

A gas water heater that will not light often has a worn igniter, but other parts can cause the same symptom. Because gas is involved, this guide focuses on telling the causes apart and on the safety rules that decide when a plumber should take over.

By the Rolling Meadows 24/7 Plumbing team · Updated November 2026

    Gas Safety Comes First

    If you smell gas, do not touch any switches. Leave the building and call 911 or the gas utility from outside. Do not try to light or repair anything. A gas smell is an emergency that overrides every other step in this guide.

    Carbon monoxide is also a risk with gas appliances. A working CO alarm near sleeping areas and near the heater is basic protection. If the alarm sounds, get outside and call 911.

    How Igniters Work

    Many modern gas water heaters use an electronic igniter, either a hot surface type that glows or a spark type that clicks. It lights the pilot or the burner, and a flame sensor confirms the flame. If the igniter fails, the burner never lights, and you get no hot water.

    Older heaters use a standing pilot lit by a flame, with a thermocouple that senses it. A pilot that will not stay lit often points to a worn thermocouple or a dirty pilot tube and not an igniter.

    What a Plumber Does

    1. 1

      Safety check

      The plumber checks for gas leaks and verifies venting and combustion air.

    2. 2

      Test the igniter

      Electrical tests show whether the igniter is receiving power and is working.

    3. 3

      Inspect related parts

      The flame sensor, gas valve and control board are checked.

    4. 4

      Replace and test

      A failed part is replaced, and the heater is run through a full cycle.

    Simple Checks You Can Do Safely

    • Make sure the gas control is set to on and the temperature is above the lowest setting
    • Check that the power switch or plug is on if your model needs electricity
    • Look for an error code flashing on the control
    • Confirm that other gas appliances work
    • Stop and call if you smell gas or see soot

    Repair or Replace the Heater

    An igniter is a small part, so a repair makes sense when the tank is sound. If the tank leaks, the heater is very old or repairs keep piling up, replacement may be the better value. Our water heater repair page has the checklist.

    If replacement is the choice, read our water heater replacement page for what to expect. A replacement involves a permit in Rolling Meadows, and the plumber handles the paperwork.

    Why the Control Board Matters

    In heaters with electronic controls, a board sends power to the igniter and watches the flame sensor. A failing board can mimic a failed igniter, so replacing the igniter first sometimes fixes nothing. A plumber tests voltage and continuity to find the real fault.

    Error codes help. Many controls blink a pattern that matches a table in the manual, and noting the pattern before the plumber arrives can shorten the visit and point to the right part.

    Venting and Draft Problems

    A heater that lights and then shuts down may have a venting problem. A blocked flue, a bird nest or a negative-pressure house can interfere with the exhaust. These can create carbon monoxide hazards, so they deserve a proper check.

    Keep the area around the heater free of paint cans, rags and other stored items. Never block the air openings in a utility closet, since the burner needs air to run safely.

    What The Visit Will Cost You In Time

    Most igniter and sensor repairs take a short visit when the part is on the truck. If the control board or gas valve needs ordering, it can take longer, and the plumber may suggest a temporary plan for hot water.

    Ask if the part is in stock and what the warranty on the repair is. If the heater is old, it is reasonable to ask for a replacement quote at the same time, so you can decide with both numbers in hand.

    Keep A CO Alarm Current

    Carbon monoxide alarms wear out, usually after five to ten years, and the date is printed on the back. Replace an expired alarm even if it still beeps. Test it monthly, and place one near sleeping areas and one near the heater.

    A CO alarm is the one safety device that protects you from a hazard you cannot smell. It costs little and is worth checking whenever you service a gas appliance.

    Rolling Meadows Rules That Touch Gas Heaters

    The City of Rolling Meadows plumbing FAQ states that concealed plumbing must have an access door or panel. That matters for a heater in a closet, because the plumber needs to reach the controls and the valves. Keep the access clear of stored items.

    The city also says a permit is required for a new or replacement water heater, and lists vent clearances of 6 inches for single-wall pipe and 1 inch for B-vent. Replacing only an igniter is a repair, and the city is the source to ask about your specific job.
