Basement Sump Pump Installation Cost in Rolling Meadows, IL

A sump pump installation price depends on whether a pit already exists, which pump you choose, whether a backup is added and how the water is discharged. This guide breaks the price into its parts, so you can compare quotes on equal terms.

By the Rolling Meadows 24/7 Plumbing team · Updated November 2026

    New Pit or Replacement

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit is the simpler and less costly job. Installing a new pit means cutting the floor, digging, setting a basin and patching the concrete. If water is seeping at a particular spot, the pit is placed where it collects.

    A plumber should explain why the pit is where it is and how the discharge will work. Ask whether the price includes concrete patching and whether the work needs a permit.

    Pump Type and Size

    Submersible pumps sit in the pit and run quietly, and pedestal pumps stand above it and are easier to service. A larger motor moves more water but is not always better, since an oversized pump cycles too often. The right size depends on the pit and how fast water arrives.

    Ask about the check valve and the switch type. A float that can jam, or a check valve that fails, causes many of the callbacks plumbers see. Quality parts cost more at the start and often less over time.

    • Ask the plumber about alarms that warn of high water

    Why A Cheap Pump Can Cost More

    A low-priced pump may have a plastic switch, a thin motor housing or a short warranty. Pumps run in a harsh environment, and a failure during a storm is the costly kind. A better pump with a reliable float and a durable check valve often pays back through fewer failures.

    Ask the plumber what brand and model is proposed, how long the warranty lasts and what it covers. Labor warranty matters as much as the part warranty, because most problems come from installation details.

    Maintenance That Protects the Investment

    Once a season, pour a bucket of water into the pit and watch the pump start, empty the pit and shut off. Clear debris from the pit and make sure the discharge outlet is free of ice, leaves and kinks.

    Replace a battery backup on the maker's schedule. A pump that is tested regularly is far more likely to run when the storm arrives, and the plumber can show you the checks in a few minutes.

    Getting Quotes You Can Compare

    Ask each plumber to quote the same scope, including the pump model, the check valve, the discharge line, floor patching and any backup. Quotes that differ in scope cannot be compared by the bottom line alone.

    Ask when the work can start, since storm season books up. A plumber who can come soon, explain the options and give a clear price before work starts is worth more than a slightly lower number from someone you cannot reach.

    Warranty And Service Questions

    Ask what the warranty covers and for how long, and who comes out if the pump stops during a storm. A short call-back promise and a clear service plan are worth considering when you compare prices.

    Keep the model and installation date. A label on the basin or a note in your file helps the next plumber know what is in the pit without digging.

    If the pit is shallow or the basin is cracked, the plumber may suggest replacing the basin along with the pump. That adds to the price but removes a weak point, and it is worth asking about when a pump is more than a decade old.

    Before storm season, ask the plumber to show you how to test the pump, where the discharge ends and which breaker feeds it. Ten minutes of instruction at the end of the job is part of what a good installer provides.

    Storm Water Facts for Rolling Meadows

    The City of Rolling Meadows operates over 54 miles of storm sewer that drains to Salt Creek, and the city maintains Plum Grove Reservoir and Twin Lakes Reservoir for storm water control. Cook County storm water is regulated under the Metropolitan Water Reclamation District Watershed Management Ordinance of 2014.

    A sump pump discharge should not send groundwater into the sanitary sewer, and local rules apply. Ask the plumber where the discharge ends and whether it is far enough from the foundation. The city is the source to confirm requirements.

    Insurance and Planning

    Many homeowners policies exclude water that backs up through a failed sump pump unless a rider is added. Ask your insurer before the next storm. If a pump fails, call a plumber quickly, since the damage grows with every hour.
