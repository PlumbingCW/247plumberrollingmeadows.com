Toilet Bowl Repair, Do It Yourself, in Rolling Meadows, IL

Many toilet problems are inexpensive to fix at home, including a running tank, a loose seat and a worn flapper. Others, such as a cracked bowl or a failed seal under the toilet, need more care. This guide sorts the jobs by difficulty and says when a plumber is the better choice.

By the Rolling Meadows 24/7 Plumbing team · Updated November 2026

    Toilet Fixes by Difficulty

    Loose or wobbly seatEasyTighten or replace the hardware

    Fixing a Running Toilet

    A toilet that runs on usually has a flapper that no longer seals, a chain that is too tight or a fill valve that needs adjusting. Lift the tank lid and watch. If water trickles into the bowl, the flapper leaks. If water rises to the overflow tube, the fill valve is set too high.

    To test the flapper, add food coloring to the tank and wait ten minutes without flushing. Color in the bowl proves the leak. Shut the supply valve, flush to empty the tank and swap the flapper with one that matches your model.

    Replacing a Flapper

    1. 1

      Shut off the supply

      Turn the valve behind the toilet clockwise and flush to drain the tank.

    2. 2

      Remove the old flapper

      Unhook the chain and slide the flapper off its pegs.

    3. 3

      Fit the new part

      Snap the new flapper in place and attach the chain with a little slack.

    4. 4

      Refill and test

      Open the valve, let the tank fill and flush twice to check the seal.

    A Leak at the Base Is Different

    Water around the base of the toilet means the seal under it has failed, the bolts are loose or the flange is damaged. The fix involves lifting the toilet and resetting it with a new wax ring. That is doable at home, but the toilet is heavy and the flange can hide damage.

    If the flange is cracked or sits below the finished floor, a repair ring or new flange is needed. Leaks that go unfixed can rot the subfloor. Our toilet repair page explains how a plumber handles it.

    Tools and Parts to Have Ready

    • Adjustable wrench and a bucket
    • Sponge, towels and plastic gloves
    • A matching flapper or fill valve
    • A new wax ring or seal and closet bolts for base work
    • Plumber's tape and a new supply line if needed

    Choosing The Right Part

    Toilet parts are not universal. Flappers come in different sizes, and a fill valve must match the tank height. Take a photo of the inside of the tank and the model number, usually stamped inside the tank or under the lid, when you shop.

    Many hardware stores sell universal kits, and they work for a lot of tanks. Buy quality parts. A cheap flapper that curls after a few months will have you doing the same repair again.

    Safety When Lifting a Toilet

    A toilet can weigh as much as 100 pounds, so lift with a partner and bend your knees. Set it on a towel to protect the floor. Stuff a rag in the flange opening to block sewer gas while the toilet is off.

    Do not overtighten the closet bolts. Porcelain can crack, and a cracked toilet has to be replaced. Alternate between the bolts in small turns until the toilet is snug and level.

    When Replacing The Toilet Is Smarter

    If the bowl has hairline cracks, if the glaze is badly stained or if you have replaced parts repeatedly, a new toilet may save money and water. Modern models flush well with less water and are easier to clean.

    Measure the rough-in distance from the wall to the center of the drain before shopping. Most toilets are twelve inches, but older homes differ. A plumber can install a new unit, test it and haul the old one away.

    Know When To Stop

    If a bolt will not turn, if the porcelain is cracked or if the water will not shut off, stop. Forcing a rusted bolt can break the base, and a valve that will not close needs a plumber to replace it before you do anything else.

    A small repair that turns into a flood is the main risk of DIY plumbing. Knowing where the main shutoff is, and having a plumber's number saved, turns a bad moment into a manageable one.

    When To Stop and Call

    Call if water keeps rising, if the toilet leaks at the base after you reset it, if the floor feels soft or if you cannot find the shutoff. A plumber can also check for hidden leaks and for the cause of repeat problems.
