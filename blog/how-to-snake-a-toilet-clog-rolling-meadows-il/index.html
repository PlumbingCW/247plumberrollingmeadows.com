How to Snake a Toilet Clog in Rolling Meadows, IL

A plunger fails sometimes, and the next tool is a toilet auger, often called a closet auger. It is made to clear the trap inside the toilet without scratching porcelain. This guide walks through using one, the common mistakes and the signs that the clog is deeper than any hand tool can reach.

By the Rolling Meadows 24/7 Plumbing team · Updated November 2026

    Choose the Right Tool

    A standard drain snake can scratch the bowl and may not turn the tight bend in a toilet trap. A closet auger has a curved tube and a rubber sleeve that protect the porcelain and guide the cable through. It is inexpensive and worth having at home.

    Gloves, old towels and a bucket make the job less messy. Put towels around the base of the toilet, since some water usually splashes. Do not pour chemical drain cleaner into a toilet before using an auger. Caustic liquid splashes back and hurts.

    How To Use a Closet Auger

    1. 1

      Retract the cable

      Pull the cable fully into the tube so the head is inside the sleeve.

    2. 2

      Seat the tip

      Place the curved end into the bowl opening so the rubber sleeve sits in the drain.

    3. 3

      Crank and push

      Turn the handle clockwise while pressing gently forward. Feel for resistance as the head reaches the clog.

    4. 4

      Break or hook the clog

      Keep turning to break it up or snag it. Pull the cable back slowly, since it may bring the blockage with it.

    5. 5

      Flush to test

      Flush once. If the water drains steadily, flush a second time and confirm the clog is gone.

    Mistakes That Make a Clog Worse

    Forcing the cable hard can scratch porcelain or push the clog further down, where it is harder to reach. Let the cable do the work and back off if you meet a hard stop. A hard stop could be a foreign object like a toy, which an auger can wedge in.

    Another mistake is flushing repeatedly while the bowl is full. Each flush adds water that may overflow. If the bowl rises after the first flush, stop, close the supply valve behind the toilet and use the auger without adding water.

    After the Clog Is Gone

    • Flush a couple of times to confirm steady drainage
    • Wipe and sanitize the area and tools
    • Keep wipes, paper towels and hygiene products out of the toilet
    • Use only toilet paper in normal amounts
    • Note if clogs repeat, since that can signal a deeper problem

    What Usually Causes Toilet Clogs

    Most toilet clogs come from too much paper, wipes labelled flushable that do not break down, hygiene products and small objects dropped in by children. Low-flow toilets with weak flushes also clog more often when the rim jets are partly blocked by mineral buildup.

    If the same toilet clogs every few weeks, the cause may be in the trap or the pipe beyond it. A plumber can pull the toilet and look, or run a camera, which shows whether a ridge, a root or an old repair is catching paper.

    Plunger First, Auger Second

    A flange plunger, which has an extra rubber collar, seals better in a toilet than a flat cup plunger. Push gently at first to avoid splashing, then pump steadily for about a dozen strokes. Many clogs break up this way and the auger is never needed.

    If the water drains slowly after plunging, wait and try again before reaching for the auger. A slow drain after a clog usually means part of the blockage remains, and the auger can clear what the plunger loosened.

    If The Auger Brings Back An Object

    Sometimes the cable returns with a toy, a brush head or a wad of wipes. That is good news, since it explains the clog. Flush a few times to confirm the line is clear, and keep small items away from the bathroom.

    If you suspect an object but cannot retrieve it, stop. Repeated pushing can lodge it in the bend. A plumber can remove the toilet and recover the object without damaging the fixture or the pipe.

    Keeping Toilets Clear Afterward

    Use a moderate amount of toilet paper and keep wipes out of the bowl, even those labelled flushable. A small wastebasket next to the toilet gives everyone an easy place for products that should not be flushed.

    Once a year, pour a bucket of hot water into the bowl to clear light buildup, and inspect the flapper and fill valve. Small habits and a little attention keep a toilet from becoming an emergency call.

    The Sewer Line Rules in Rolling Meadows

    The City of Rolling Meadows sewer guide says the property owner owns the sewer lateral from the building to the main. The city asks residents to call Public Works before calling a plumber when a blockage may be in the main, and the city checks the main at no cost. That call can save you a plumber bill if the problem is on the city side.

    The guide also recommends a 6 inch cleanout on the lateral and rodding every two to three years with a 6 inch root cutter, because roots enter at joints. If you have a cleanout, a plumber can use it to clear a line that a toilet auger cannot reach.

    When to Call a Plumber

    Call if the auger does not clear the toilet, if more than one fixture is affected or if sewage is on the floor. A plumber can run a camera and cable through the cleanout, which reaches farther than any home tool.
