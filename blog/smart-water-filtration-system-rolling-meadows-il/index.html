Smart Water Filtration Systems for Rolling Meadows, IL Homes

A smart water filtration system adds sensors and a connected app to a filter, so it can report flow, filter life and sometimes water quality readings. Before you buy one, it helps to know what your water already contains and what the extra features actually do for you.

By the Rolling Meadows 24/7 Plumbing team · Updated November 2026

    What Makes a Filter Smart

    Most smart systems add flow meters and an app that tracks usage, tells you when to change cartridges and may alert you to leaks. Some include sensors for total dissolved solids or pressure. They do not add new kinds of filtration. The filter media is still carbon, sediment, reverse osmosis or similar.

    That means the first question is what you want removed. A smart label on a carbon filter does not make it remove contaminants that carbon cannot. Check what the filter is certified to reduce and by whom.

    Test Before You Buy

    A lab test or a quality home test kit tells you what is at your tap. Ask the city for its annual water quality report, which lists what the supplier tests for and the results. Spend money on equipment only after you know what problem you are solving.

    Taste and odor complaints often come from chlorine or from household plumbing, and cloudy water can come from air in the lines. A plumber can check pressure, fixtures and the heater, which are sometimes the real source.

    Questions Before Buying a Smart Filter

    • What contaminants is it certified to reduce?
    • How often are cartridges replaced, and what do they cost?
    • Does the app work without a subscription?
    • What happens to the filter if the Wi-Fi or app fails?
    • Who handles service if a connection or valve fails?

    Whole-House or Point-of-Use

    A whole-house filter treats all the water entering the home, which protects fixtures and appliances and improves the taste everywhere. A point-of-use filter, such as one under the kitchen sink, treats only that tap and is cheaper to buy and service.

    For drinking water concerns, a certified point-of-use filter often does the job. For sediment or chlorine complaints at every tap, a whole-house unit makes more sense. A plumber can help match the type to the problem you actually have.

    Cartridges, Costs and Connectivity

    Over several years, cartridges and any subscription fees can cost more than the unit. Ask for the replacement schedule and prices before you buy, and check whether the app keeps working if you cancel a subscription.

    Connected devices also need a reliable network and a power supply. A filter that shuts water off when it loses connection would be a poor design, so ask what the unit does if the connection fails.

    Installation Details That Matter

    A filter should have shutoff valves on both sides, a bypass and room to change cartridges. The plumber should support the housing so its weight does not strain the pipes. Pressure gauges before and after the filter help you see when cartridges are clogged.

    Check the system after installation and again after a few weeks. Small leaks at new fittings often appear after the first pressure cycles, and a follow-up check catches them early.

    Maintenance You Can Expect

    Plan to change sediment and carbon cartridges on the schedule the maker gives. Sanitize the housing when you change them, and keep spare cartridges on hand. A filter left in place too long can slow flow and even harbor bacteria.

    Check the pressure drop across the filter now and then. A big drop means the cartridge is loaded, and an app can alert you, though a simple gauge does the same job.

    Ask the installer where the shutoff is for the filter and what to do if it leaks. A bypass lets you keep water flowing while you replace a cartridge, and knowing where it is turns a leak into a quick fix.

    Write the install date and the next cartridge change on the housing with a marker, or set a calendar reminder. Even a smart app can miss a notice, and a visible date is a good backup that anyone in the house can read.
