Pump Not Working and Water Rising

Do not step into standing water if the basement outlets, panel or appliances may be energized. If the water is near electrical equipment, stay out and call the electric utility or 911. If it is safe, check whether the pump has power, whether the float moves freely and whether the discharge line is clear.

Move belongings off the floor and photograph the water for your insurer. A sump pump failure is time sensitive, and the sooner a pump or backup is running, the less cleanup you will face.

Why Sump Pumps Matter Here

Rolling Meadows sits along Salt Creek, and the city maintains more than 54 miles of storm sewer that drains toward it. The city also operates storm water facilities, including Plum Grove Reservoir and Twin Lakes Reservoir, to manage heavy rain. Storms that stress those systems are also the ones that load a household sump pump.

The city's guidance for standing water is to wait up to 48 hours for it to drain before treating it as a storm sewer problem. A basement is a private matter, though, and a sump pump is the home's own defense. If your pump is old, noisy or cycling constantly, it is worth testing before the next heavy storm.

Battery Backup and Power Outages

Storms that overload a pump often knock out power at the same time. A battery backup pump, or a water-powered backup where it is permitted and the water supply is adequate, can keep a basement dry during an outage. Backups need testing and eventual battery replacement.

Ask the plumber about the right size and a plan for testing. A backup that is never tested may not work when needed. The basement waterproofing page covers other ways to manage groundwater.

Discharge: Where the Water Goes

A sump pump discharge should send water away from the foundation and not into the sanitary sewer. Pumping groundwater into a sanitary sewer is generally restricted because it overloads the sewer system, and local rules apply. The Metropolitan Water Reclamation District's Watershed Management Ordinance of 2014 is the regional framework for storm water in Cook County.

Ask the plumber where your discharge goes and whether it ends far enough from the house. A line that freezes in winter or that dumps water beside the foundation will undo the pump's work.
