Sewer Backup: What to Do First

Stop all water use, including laundry and dishwashers. Do not flush toilets. Every fixture drains to the same line, and anything you add can come up in the lowest drain in the house.

Then make the right first call. Rolling Meadows Public Works can check the city main at no cost, and its number is 847-963-0500 on weekdays, with a non-emergency police line at 847-255-2416 after hours. If the main is clear, the problem is on your side of the property line, and that is where we come in.

The city's guidance says the owner's responsibility ends where the lateral meets the main. Knowing that boundary can save you from paying to clear a blockage that was never on your property.

Signs of a Damaged Sewer Line

Repeat clogs in the lowest drains

A broken or sagging pipe collects debris at the same spot, so the clog keeps returning after cabling.

Sewage smell in the yard or basement

A cracked line can leak into soil or release gas near the foundation.

Soggy or sunken lawn patches

Wastewater escaping from a crack can soften the ground above the pipe.

Gurgling and slow drains throughout

A partial collapse or heavy root mass restricts the whole line.

How We Find the Break

A sewer repair starts with a camera. A small waterproof camera on a flexible cable travels the line and records cracks, roots, bellies and offset joints, and shows how far from the cleanout the problem is. Our sewer inspection page explains the process and what you can ask to see.

Seeing the line matters because it changes the repair. A single root-filled joint may need a spot fix, while a long run of cracked clay pipe may need lining or replacement. A plumber who guesses without a camera can dig in the wrong place.

Roots, Age and Rolling Meadows Soil

Rolling Meadows has mature trees on many residential streets, and the city's sewer guide says tree roots can grow into open pipe joints and that large trees over the lateral should be disclosed to the contractor. Roots enter at a joint, then thicken and trap debris, and the line backs up more often each year.

Homes built between 1950 and 1980, which is most of the housing here, may have older lateral materials that have been in the ground a long time. A camera shows the actual condition, and that tells you whether a cleaning is enough or a repair is worth planning before the next backup.

Permits and the Right of Way

The city's guide says a city permit is required before most sewer work, and it must be obtained before the work begins. It also says the city will not start a right-of-way repair until a six-inch cleanout is installed or rodding has been attempted, and that blockages found in the right of way are invoiced to the owner if the city's contractor clears them.

Because the rules affect timing and cost, we explain which permits apply before work starts. Then you can decide on the repair with the full picture instead of finding out about a permit mid-job.
