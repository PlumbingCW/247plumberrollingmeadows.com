What Sewer Line Cleaning Means

Sewer line cleaning clears the pipe that carries waste from your house to the city main. Unlike a single drain cleaning, it works on the large line that every fixture shares. A plumber reaches it through the cleanout, a six-inch access point with a cap that is usually near the foundation or in the basement.

The machine used depends on the clog. A heavy cable with a root cutter handles roots and solid blockages, and a hydro jetter cleans grease and scale off the wall. Most calls start with a cable to get the line moving, then move to jetting if the pipe is coated.

Active Sewer Backup: Your First Steps

If sewage is on the floor, stop using water, keep people and pets out and avoid touching the water, since sewage carries bacteria. Open windows if the smell is strong and call the 24/7 line.

Check the cleanout if you know where it is, because it often tells the plumber what to expect. Do not remove the cap if water is standing in the line, since pressure behind it can push sewage out.

Call the City First, Then Us

Rolling Meadows publishes a sanitary sewer guide that asks homeowners with a backup to call the city before hiring a contractor.

Keeping the Line Open Afterward

The city's guidance describes routine maintenance as rodding from an outside six-inch cleanout with a six-inch root cutter by a plumbing contractor every two to three years, or as the line's history dictates. If your line has cleared roots before, that schedule can be worth following.

Some homeowners also pair a cleaning with a camera inspection so they can see the pipe before and after. Ask dispatch about both when you call, and decide what fits your home and budget.

Longer Laterals and Rolling Meadows Properties

The city notes that if a service line is longer than about 100 feet, the contractor's equipment has to be able to handle that length, and that if the rod cannot reach the main, the city should be called before it is removed. Those are good questions to ask any plumber, and we will tell you how far our machine reaches before the visit.

In neighborhoods near Salt Creek and in homes built between 1950 and 1980, a lateral may cross a lawn with mature trees. That is exactly where a cleaning and a follow-up camera run give you the clearest picture. Owners in the city's original neighborhoods can read more on the plumber in Kimball Hill page.
